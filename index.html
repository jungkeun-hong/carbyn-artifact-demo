The $20B Connected-Worker Dilemma

The category raised, scaled, and priced itself around one assumption: that a worker will stop, find a screen, and read. The buyer pays per station, per seat, or per site. The technician pays with their hands.

The Screen-Bound Trap

Every screen-first tool assumes an interaction that the physical job forbids. The failure is not the software quality. It is the posture the software demands.

Greasy gloves, dead glass

Nitrile plus hydraulic oil equals a capacitive screen that ignores you. The real workflow becomes: strip glove, wipe hand, tap, re-glove. Four actions to read one torque spec. Most techs stop opening the app by week three.

Line-of-sight hazard

Eyes down on a tablet next to live 480V, a pinch point, or a moving conveyor is an incident report waiting to be written. AR headsets make this worse, not better: an overlay in the visual field is an occlusion in a hazard zone. Carbyn ships no HUD on purpose.

Wrench time death by a thousand taps

Walk to the kiosk, log in, search, scroll, walk back, forget step 6, walk again. Then close the work order at shift end from memory, badly. The SOP was perfect. The retrieval cost ate the day.
